### Contribution/Goal

The most important part of this document is to identify what you want to achieve, and how you will claim you have achieved it.

**GenAI use (optional)**

My suggestion is to do some brain-storming / mind-mapping on paper first, and decide on the ideas _you_ find interesting.
Then use genAI as a sounding board: prompt it first to critique and be pedantic about your ideas, then explain what you're thinking of, and have it poke holes and ask you for clarifications.

If you already have a team (or any other willing friends), even better is to do this first with other humans, then with the AI, and reflect on how it was different, how it was similar, and how you will approach ideation and project-synthesis moving forward.

**For MAE 5110 Students**

Your proposals need to be centered around a contribution; you may come up with something new (incl. from your own research, if feasible), you may also reproduce an existing paper.
If you choose to reproduce an existing paper, the proposal should be very clear on what aspects of the paper you wish to reproduce, and how you will validate that the reproduction is meaningful.

In the next iteration, you will need to also start "fencing in" your contribution, giving context to _why_ your achievement will be a contribution. Remember, contributions should be about insight, not novelty. A thorough benchmark that reproduces existing work, but makes clearer _why_ or _how_ to achieve that performance gives insight with novelty.

This will require _some_ understanding of the literature (for the scope of the class, identify at least one relevant paper), and a clear enough contribution.
This [talk by by MIT professor Patrick Winston on how to speak](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Unzc731iCUY) actually includes excellent nuggets on how to write, and how to think about research overall, especially the importance of "fencing in" your contribution.
I strongly recommend you watch it.

### Tips

It is, in my experience, typical of early graduate students to aim for a lofty and vague contribution that would actually take multiple PhDs to achieve.
This is often attributed to the [Dunning-Kruger](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1999-15054-002) effect.
Be mindful that you only have about half a semester semester to implement, validate, and test the project.

While exploring project ideas, it is therefore important to scope it very cleanly; decide what you want to focus on, and then try to triage and eliminate anything that isn't needed to demonstrate that contribution. With the caveat that the course still requires you to attempt a sim-to-real transfer onto the Go2.

You may propose up to three projects here; you may use this to propose three different ideas, or propose three projects that aim at different levels ambition to get feedback on what we (the course staff) think is reasonable, or some mix of the two.
